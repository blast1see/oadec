# Evidence, 2026-09-09: the coding tools the film corpus never used

The corpus of 2026-09-08 covered coupling, rematrixing, block switching,
dither and skip fields, but no stream in it used the adaptive hybrid
transform (AHT) or spectral extension (SPX). Both are implemented from
ETSI TS 102 366 Annex E and both were untested. This is how the material
was made, what it found and where the paths stand now.

## Making material for AHT and spectral extension

Encoders reach for these tools when the data rate is low. The Dolby
Encoding Engine's `pcm_to_ddp` filter (template
`wav_pcm_to_ddp_ec3.xml`) encoded the same 5.1 content at four rates; the
input was the 24-bit WAVE the Dolby decoder produced from
`pi-head-dee.ec3`, so the audio is identical to material already in the
corpus and only the encoding differs.

| Data rate | Result | Coding tools reported by `oadec info` |
|---|---|---|
| 96 kbit/s | DEE refuses (exit 10) | — |
| 128 kbit/s | DEE refuses (exit 10) | — |
| 192 kbit/s | 2,538,240 bytes | coupling, **spectral extension** (3,305 of 3,305 frames), **AHT** (3,289 frames), transient pre-noise, block switching, dither, skip fields |
| 384 kbit/s | 5,076,480 bytes | coupling, **AHT** (3,276 frames), block switching, dither, skip fields |

The two streams are kept in the work directory as
`ec3/pi-head-spx192.ec3` and `ec3/pi-head-aht384.ec3`.

## What it found: a crash in the gain-adaptive quantizer

The first AHT frame panicked the mantissa reader (index out of bounds).
Cause: the helper array of clause E.2.4.2 marks every bin whose `hebap`
falls inside the mode's range as gain-coded, but clause E.2.4.4.2 says
`gaqmod == 0` transmits **no** gain words at all and uses the plain
quantizer throughout. The reader looked one up regardless. Fixed; a
missing gain word is now a typed syntax error instead of a panic, and
both streams decode with 0 errors and 0 CRC failures at 185–252x real
time.

## Are the two paths right? Three decoders on the same streams

`tools/three_way.py`, whole clip (105.8 s), signal-to-difference ratios in
dB. Higher is closer; the verdict is whether oadec sits at least as close
to the Dolby decode as FFmpeg does.

**192 kbit/s (AHT + spectral extension + transient pre-noise)**

| Channel | Dolby vs FFmpeg | oadec vs FFmpeg | oadec vs Dolby |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| L | 18.6 | 24.7 | **19.1** |
| R | 18.7 | 24.5 | **19.3** |
| C | 34.9 | 34.5 | **39.5** |
| LFE | 49.8 | 49.8 | **88.0** |
| Ls | 15.1 | 22.3 | **15.4** |
| Rs | 14.8 | 21.5 | **15.2** |

**384 kbit/s (AHT, no spectral extension)**

| Channel | Dolby vs FFmpeg | oadec vs FFmpeg | oadec vs Dolby |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| L | 31.2 | 35.7 | **32.8** |
| R | 31.5 | 35.5 | **33.3** |
| C | 44.2 | 43.8 | **49.4** |
| LFE | 78.0 | 78.6 | **87.3** |
| Ls | 26.7 | 29.2 | **28.7** |
| Rs | 26.3 | 28.9 | **28.3** |

oadec is closer to the Dolby decode than FFmpeg is on every channel of
both streams. The LFE is the cleanest reading of the AHT path, because it
carries neither dither nor spectral extension: it is AHT-coded in both
streams and lands 88.0 and 87.3 dB from the Dolby decode, against
FFmpeg's 49.8 and 78.0.

The absolute numbers are lower than for the film corpus (35–55 dB) because
spectral extension fills the high bands with pseudo-random noise whose
sequence, like the dither of clause 6.3.4, is the implementation's own.
Three decoders therefore cannot agree there by construction, which is why
the envelope and not the absolute ratio is the test.

## Still not exercised

- **Enhanced coupling** (ECPL): parsed so that frames stay in sync, not
  decoded. No stream in the corpus uses it and DEE offers no way to ask
  for it.
- **Transient pre-noise processing**: the 192 kbit/s stream signals it and
  oadec parses its fields, but the post-process itself is not applied
  (FFmpeg does not apply it either).

> **Superseded (0.2.0).** Both are decoded: enhanced coupling on material made
> with `oadec eac3-ecpl-inject`, transient pre-noise processing in the decoder
> (`--ecpl-spec` and `--no-tpnp` exist to measure them); see `2026-09-09-b.md`
> and `docs/eac3.md`.

## Regression cover

Three tests were added to the opt-in real-media suite
(`crates/oadec-cli/tests/real.rs`):

- the two low-rate clips must decode cleanly and must still report AHT and
  spectral extension, so the material cannot silently go missing;
- every AC-3 family stream in the corpus must decode with no failure of
  any kind, and the union of their coding tools must still contain
  coupling, rematrixing, block switching, dither, skip fields, AHT and
  spectral extension;
- every JOC stream must carry an EMDF container with both metadata
  payloads in every frame, all parsing to the byte.

The last of those failed on the first run, on `disclosure-web.ec3`: seven
of its 272,903 frames appeared to hold a broken container. They do not.
The skip-field scan looks for the sync word `0x5838`, and in those seven
frames the byte pair also occurs inside the payload of the real container.
Every frame of that stream carries a container that parses, with both
payloads. The scan now counts a failure only when a frame yields no
container at all, and reports the stray sync words separately: 272,903
containers, 0 frames without one, 7 false sync words.

They need a valid JSON report, which the AC-3 family reports were not: two
fields were printed with Rust's Debug formatter. The report is now built
with `serde_json` and carries the same `clean` flag and `failures` object
as the TrueHD report.

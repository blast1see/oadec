# Evidence — enhanced coupling, transient pre-noise, clip gain (2026-09-09)

Three tools were parsed but not decoded, and the notes said so: enhanced
coupling had no material anywhere, transient pre-noise processing was
signalled but not applied, and the JOC clip gain was reported because the
standard never says what to do with it. All three are decided here, each
against a Dolby decoder or a controlled Dolby encode.

Material and tools: `E:\oadec-work`, Dolby Encoding Engine 5.2.1
(`C:\dee`), Dolby Reference Player 3.2.0, FFmpeg 2026-09-02. Specifications
under `E:\oadec-work\specs\`: ETSI TS 102 366 V1.2.1 and V1.4.1, ATSC
A/52:2012 and A/52:2018, ETSI TS 103 420 V1.2.1.

---

## 1. The standards disagree about enhanced coupling

ETSI TS 102 366 V1.4.1, which the E-AC-3 core was written from, carries a
reduced enhanced coupling. Its clause E.2.5.5 has three subclauses
(introduction, amplitude, coefficient generation) and the whole process is

```
chmant[ch][bin] = ecplmant[bin] * ampbnd[ch][sbnd];
```

Its audblk table prints the coordinate field as `reserved 9 x (necplbnd - 1)`.

The other three editions have four subclauses and a complex process:

| | V1.4.1 | V1.2.1 | A/52:2012 | A/52:2018 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| carrier reconstruction | absent | E.2.5.5.1 | E.3.5.5.1 | E.3.5.5.1 |
| amplitude | E.2.5.5.1 | E.2.5.5.2 | E.3.5.5.2 | E.3.5.5.2 |
| angle and chaos | absent | E.2.5.5.3 | E.3.5.5.3 | E.3.5.5.3 |
| coefficient generation | E.2.5.5.2 | E.2.5.5.4 | E.3.5.5.4 | E.3.5.5.4 |
| coordinate field | `9 x (necplbnd - 1)` reserved | `9 x necplbnd` named | `9 x necplbnd` named | `9 x necplbnd` named |

The three that describe the full process agree word for word. The parser now
reads `9 x necplbnd`: three documents against one, and the shorter count
would desynchronise the rest of the block.

## 2. Material for a tool nothing emits

No stream in the corpus uses enhanced coupling (0 of 17, `corpus_compare.log`),
DEE's `pcm_to_ddp` schema has no switch for it, and FFmpeg's `eac3dec.c` says
in its own comment that no known sample exists. So the material was built
here, in oadec, with no third-party code.

`oadec eac3-ecpl-inject` rewrites a stream's standard coupling as enhanced
coupling. It works because the two regions share a coefficient grid exactly:

| | standard | enhanced |
|---|---|---|
| first bin | `37 + 12*cplbegf` | `ecplsubbndtab[ecpl_begin_subbnd]`, and `ecplsubbndtab[4+k] = 37+12k` |
| last bin | `37 + 12*(cplendf+3)` | `ecplsubbndtab[ecplendf+7]`, the same expression |

so `ecpl_begin_subbnd = cplbegf + 4` and `ecplendf = cplendf` land on the same
bins and every exponent, bit allocation and mantissa stays untouched. Only the
strategy field, the coordinates, the frame size and the frame check change.

Source `ec3/pi-head-aht384.ec3` (5.1, 384 kbit/s, coupling in every frame, no
spectral extension, `cplbegf = 6`, `cplendf = 10`, so bins 109 to 193).

```
$ oadec eac3-ecpl-inject ec3/pi-head-aht384.ec3 -o ecpl/pi-head-ecpl.ec3 --chaos 7
enhanced coupling injected into 3305 of 3305 frames (0 copied unchanged, 0 sync errors, 0 bytes skipped)

$ oadec verify ecpl/pi-head-ecpl.ec3 --json
clean True   ecpl_frames 3305   decode_errors 0   crc_failures 0   samples 5076480
coverage: coupling, enhanced-coupling, aht, block-switching, dither, skip-fields
```

The frame count and the sample count are those of the source. Both Dolby
decoders accept the file.

## 3. What Dolby actually implements

Two streams from the same source, one with every `ecplangle` and `ecplchaos`
zero (`--flat-angle --chaos 0`), one with a full spread of both:

| decoder | zero angles vs full spread |
|---|---|
| DEE 5.2.1 `ddp_decode` | **bit-identical** |
| Dolby Reference Player 3.2.0 | **bit-identical** |

Neither implements the angle or the chaos. oadec therefore defaults to the
amplitude-only reading and offers the ATSC process behind `--ecpl-spec`.

Against the Dolby decode, in the default reading, per channel over the whole
file:

```
 channel   lag    gain   signal     diff     SNR
       L   256  0.9999    -29.8    -60.7    30.9
       R   256  1.0000    -29.6    -61.6    32.1
       C   256  1.0000    -33.3    -75.3    42.0
     LFE   255  1.0000    -34.8    -80.5    45.7
      Ls   256  0.9976    -41.3    -61.1    19.8
      Rs   256  0.9994    -41.0    -61.9    20.9
```

Every gain is 1,0000 and the lag is the usual 256 samples. The residual is the
dither of clause 6.3.4, which is the implementation's own, so the fair
comparison is against what the *standard* coupling path scores on the same
material. Restricted to the coupling region itself (10,2 to 18,1 kHz), where
dithered zero-bit bins dominate:

| case | L | R | C | LFE | Ls | Rs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| standard coupling, the stream as it is | 6,0 | 5,8 | 22,6 | 23,3 | -3,5 | -3,3 |
| enhanced coupling, unity amplitude | 5,2 | 3,6 | 28,5 | 23,3 | 28,7 | 28,8 |
| enhanced coupling, varying amplitude | 9,1 | 8,0 | 28,4 | 23,3 | 28,8 | 29,6 |

The enhanced coupling path sits at or above the floor the standard coupling
path already sits at. `tools/two_way.py` is the comparison; it is the
FFmpeg-free counterpart of `three_way.py`, needed because FFmpeg refuses these
streams outright.

## 4. The printed overlap-add is 6 dB low

Clause E.3.5.5.1 step 3 sums the windowed blocks without the factor two the
main body's overlap-add carries (clause 6.9.4). With one coupled channel at
unit amplitude, zero angle and zero chaos the whole chain is an identity, so
the scaling is measurable without any reference decoder:

```
as printed:  worst 4.967e-1, mean ratio 0.500000     (exactly -6,02 dB)
with the factor two: worst < 1e-9
```

`ecpl::tests::unit_amplitude_and_zero_angle_return_the_carrier` keeps it
pinned, together with tests for the angle convention (code 32 negates the
channel), the amplitude table (code 4 halves it, code 31 mutes it), a
neighbour without enhanced coupling, and a monotone fall in the correlation
between two channels as the chaos code rises.

## 5. Transient pre-noise processing

`ec3/pi-head-spx192.ec3` signals the tool in 18 of 3 305 frames, with 24
parameter sets, `transprocloc*4` from 1 196 to 2 556 samples and
`transproclen` from 0 to 255. Locations past 1 536 point into the following
frame, which is why the correction needs a buffer rather than a filter.

Decoding with and without the tool, then measuring both against the Dolby
decode over exactly the samples the tool touches:

```
 channel   samples   without      with    change
       L      9180     -38.1     -41.9     -3.79 dB
       R     10582     -42.9     -46.0     -3.05 dB
      Rs      1690     -59.6     -63.6     -4.00 dB
     total   21452     -40.4     -44.0     -3.57 dB
```

Dolby applies the tool; applying it moves oadec towards Dolby on every channel
that carries a transient. Sweeping the readings of the clause against the same
reference picked the printed structure over every alternative, and picked a
linear cross-fade over a raised cosine by 0,3 to 0,6 dB per channel. The
sample count is unchanged and `--no-tpnp` reproduces the previous output byte
for byte (`md5 e4899f00df8825517fedd7b137ee56e3`).

## 6. JOC clip gain

ETSI TS 103 420 clause 6.3.3.2 defines `joc_clipgain = (1 + y/32) 2^(x-4)`
over [1; 8,75]; the word does not appear anywhere in clause 6.6, which
specifies the whole JOC decode.

A controlled encode settles it. The Atmos master `out/pi-head/pi-head.atmos`
was encoded with DEE twice at 768 kbit/s, once as it stands and once with its
object audio scaled by exactly three. The unscaled encode carries clip gain
1,000 in all 3 305 frames; the scaled one carries 360 frames from 1,031 to
1,813. Decoding both cores and taking the per-frame energy ratio, over frames
where the scaling did not clip the source:

| clip gain | frames | hot/ref | x gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,0000 | 408 | 2,9995 | 2,9995 |
| 1,0312 | 125 | 2,9100 | 3,0009 |
| 1,0625 | 110 | 2,8234 | 2,9999 |
| 1,0938 | 30 | 2,7422 | 2,9992 |
| 1,1250 | 4 | 2,6615 | 2,9942 |
| 1,3125 | 3 | 2,2866 | 3,0011 |

The encoder divides the downmix by the clip gain. Per channel, over the 250
frames with a gain above 1,05, `(hot/ref) x gain` is 2,996 (L), 2,995 (R),
2,992 (C), 2,999 (LFE), 3,000 (Ls), 2,999 (Rs) — the LFE included.

Applying it to the object program removes the level error: decoded objects
without it sit at `3/clipgain` of the unscaled decode, with it at 3,00.
Switching the flag changes the object output by exactly the clip gain
(1,0312 measured for 1,0312, 1,8125 for 1,8125, over 26 buckets).

Two other readings are refuted:

- **not a matrix range extender** — the peak dequantized coefficient already
  reaches the 9,61 ceiling in frames with gain 1,000, and gain times peak runs
  to 18,7 in frames with a gain, well past the ceiling;
- **not unrelated to level** — on `extraction-nf.ec3` (two hours, 214 952
  frames at 1,000 and 6 303 above it) the mean core peak is 0,091 at gain
  1,000 and 0,38 to 0,74 above it, with the maximum pinned at 0,95 to 1,05 in
  every bucket.

## 7. Regression

All seventeen AC-3 family streams in the corpus still verify clean with
unchanged frame and sample counts. `pi-head-spx192.ec3` decoded with
`--no-tpnp` is byte-identical to the pre-change output. The opt-in real-media
suite gained two tests, one converting a stream to enhanced coupling and
decoding it under both readings, one checking that the transient correction
changes no sample count and that the reported parameters stay in range.

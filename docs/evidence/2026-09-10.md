# Evidence — TrueHD objects against Dolby, and what turns the JOC upmix off (2026-09-10)

Two questions were left open by `docs/evidence/2026-09-09-c.md`: the TrueHD
object presentation had no Dolby reference, and one stream in the library
carried downmix configuration 0 which the Dolby decoder would not upmix.
Both are answered here.

---

## 1. TrueHD objects, bit-exact against Dolby

The Dolby TrueHD element refuses a raw `.thd` elementary stream: it answers
`0x50` on the first access unit whether the parser is in passthrough or not.
It does not refuse the same audio in a container. `ffmpeg` writes TrueHD into
MP4 behind `-strict -2`, as an `mlpa` sample entry with the `dmlp` box, and
this build of `qtdemux` reads the samples but labels them
`audio/x-gst-fourcc-mlpa` because it does not know the entry. One
`capssetter` fixes that:

```
ffmpeg -i in.thd -c:a copy -strict -2 -movflags faststart -f mp4 in.mp4

gst-launch-1.0 filesrc location=in.mp4 ! qtdemux
  ! capssetter caps="audio/x-true-hd" replace=true join=false
  ! dlbtruehdparse enable-metadata=true
  ! dlbtruehddec out-ch-config=21 presentation=16
  ! "audio/x-raw(meta:DlbObjectAudioMeta),format=S32LE"
  ! identity ! filesink location=objects.s32
```

The decoder then reports `Playing presentation: 16ch` and writes the coded
objects as 24-bit samples in 32-bit words.

| | our channels | samples | identical samples | worst difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pi | 12 | 5 075 800 | 60 909 600 of 60 909 600 | 0 |
| Talk to Me | 16 | 1 920 000 | 30 720 000 of 30 720 000 | 0 |

Not "to the dither floor": **identical**, every sample of every object, in the
order the programme lists them, with the same sample count. TrueHD carries no
dither and no noise the decoder has to invent, so two correct decoders have
nothing left to differ about, and this is the same result the eight-channel
presentation already gave against the Dolby engine inside Plex.

Dolby pads its output to sixteen channels with digital silence when the
programme has fewer, which is why Pi comes out as twelve real channels and
four empty ones.

One clip would not go through: Shaun of the Dead answers "Selected Dolby
TrueHD presentation is not available" at `presentation=16` even though its
presentation 3 exists and carries twelve channels. Not chased.

## 2. Downmix configuration 0 turns the Dolby upmix off

`oadec eac3-joc-config` rewrites `joc_dmx_config_idx` and nothing else. The
field is the first three bits of the JOC payload; the payload sits at a bit
offset inside an EMDF container, the container at a byte offset inside a skip
field, the skip field at a bit offset in the frame. The parser now reports all
three, so the field can be reached without moving another bit. Only the frame
check has to be redone: 3 062 bytes differ over 976 frames, about three per
frame, and every payload still parses to the byte with no frame check failing.

Talk to Me, which the Dolby decoder gives sixteen objects for, relabelled from
3 to 0 and handed back to the same decoder:

| label | what the decoder says | output |
|---|---|---|
| 3, as coded | `ATMOS decoding` | 16 channels |
| 0, relabelled | `Channel-based decoding joc_enable(1), jocd_out_mode(1)` | 6 channels |

Same audio, same metadata, three bits apart. So configuration 0 is enough on
its own to stop the Dolby decoder upmixing, and the divergence recorded
yesterday is a property of that decoder rather than of the Snatch stream.

## 3. Snatch has a second reason, and it is none of the obvious ones

The reverse relabel does not hold. Snatch relabelled from 0 to 3 still comes
out as six channels, on the head of the film and on a clip from twenty-five
minutes in. Four candidates were tried and none of them is it:

- **The sequence counter.** Clause 6.3.3.3 says `joc_seq_count` is 0 on the
  first frame of a bitstream. Cutting the first frame, and then the first ten,
  off a working stream leaves no zero anywhere and the Dolby decoder still
  gives objects.
- **Splices.** The head of Snatch signals 48 of them; the clip from the middle
  signals none and is refused just the same.
- **The steep slope.** Snatch uses it for 1 200 of 19 200 object updates.
  So do Extraction, Glass Onion and Red Notice, for 30, 195 and 105, and the
  Dolby decoder gives objects for all three.
- **Structure.** The metadata scans of Snatch and Talk to Me differ in nothing
  but their frame counts: same payload ids, same object count, same band
  count, same JOC extension bytes, and the Dolby parser flags both
  `atmos=true`.

The refusal is also not a decision taken once at the start. Concatenating two
hundred frames of Talk to Me in front of the relabelled Snatch makes the
decoder open in `ATMOS decoding` and then switch to `Channel-based decoding`
when it reaches the Snatch frames, dropping from sixteen channels to six
mid-stream. Whatever it objects to is in those frames.

Our decoder follows clause 6.6, which draws no distinction, and reconstructs
the objects. That remains an open divergence, now with four explanations
eliminated.
